# Step 5: Lease and landlord consent

Landlord (per October research): J.T. Sheffield LLC, which bought the property in 2022.
Do this before spending money on anything else. An attorney should review the actual lease.

## Check the lease for
- [ ] Permitted use clause: does it allow nightly or short-term lodging, not only a restaurant?
- [ ] Assignment/sublease terms, if Burton Inn LLC is not the original tenant.
- [ ] Who is responsible for repairs, code compliance, and insurance requirements
      (additional insured, minimum limits).
- [ ] Rent structure, especially any percentage of sales.
- [ ] Term and renewal, long enough to recoup furnishing and listing costs.
- [ ] Approval rights over alterations (alarms, signage, floor-plan postings).

## Ask the landlord for, in writing
- [ ] Consent to short-term lodging in the four upstairs rooms.
- [ ] Confirmation that the rooms may be used up to the guest cap you choose.
- [ ] Any old permit paperwork or Certificate of Occupancy they hold.
- [ ] For later (Track B): consent to the liquor license, and who pays for septic and hood work.

## Draft email to the landlord
> Subject: Burton Inn, lodging use and consent
>
> Hello [name],
>
> Burton Inn LLC plans to begin renting the four upstairs guest rooms on a nightly basis
> as the first phase of reopening. Before we invest in this, could you confirm in writing
> that the lease permits nightly lodging in those rooms, and share any permit or
> Certificate of Occupancy records you hold for the building? I would also like to
> schedule a time to discuss insurance requirements and, later, the kitchen.
>
> Thank you,
> [Name]
