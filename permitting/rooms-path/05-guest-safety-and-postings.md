# Steps 6 to 9: Insurance, safety, postings, accessibility

Source: RCW 64.37 (Washington short-term rental law), read directly.
One wrinkle: RCW 64.37's definition of a short-term rental excludes a hotel, motel or
bed and breakfast, and its "dwelling unit" definition excludes a duly licensed bed and
breakfast, inn or hotel. Which side the Burton Inn falls on depends on how King County
classifies the use (zoning question 2). Following these requirements is cheap and safe
either way, so treat them as applying.

## Insurance (RCW 64.37.050)
- [ ] Primary liability insurance of at least $1,000,000 aggregate on the unit, OR book
      every stay through a platform that provides equal or greater primary coverage.
- [ ] Ask the broker whether the policy covers nightly rental of a commercial building,
      and name the landlord as additional insured if the lease requires it.

## Consumer safety (RCW 64.37.030)
- [ ] A contact who can respond to guest inquiries at the unit during the stay.
- [ ] Carbon monoxide alarms compliant with RCW 19.27.530.
- [ ] Smoke alarms in each guest room and the egress path (own check; confirm with King County).

## Post in a conspicuous place in each room (RCW 64.37.030(1)(c))
- [ ] The street address
- [ ] Emergency contact info for police, fire and EMS
- [ ] Floor plan showing fire exits and escape routes
- [ ] Maximum occupancy
- [ ] Operator or designated contact information

Enforcement: first violation gets a warning letter, later ones are class 2 civil infractions.

## Guest cap (decision D1)
- 10 guests total or fewer likely keeps the rooms in the lighter R-3 category.
- Up to 16 means R-1 with more fire and egress scrutiny.
- The posted maximum occupancy must match whatever you choose.

## Accessibility
Rooms are upstairs only. Repairs alone do not trigger upgrades. Write a short
feasibility note (why an accessible route is not feasible, cost vs. 20% rule) and keep
it in `../permits/` in case the county asks.

## Taxes (RCW 64.37.020)
Remit applicable state and local taxes unless the booking platform remits them. Platforms
register with DOR; the LLC still reports income and files returns.
