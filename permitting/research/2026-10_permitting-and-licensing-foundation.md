# Burton Inn (24007 Vashon Hwy SW): Permitting and Licensing Workbook Foundation, October 2026

Reopening the Burton Inn is feasible, but the critical path is not the liquor license: it is Public Health's full food plan review (required because the kitchen has been closed more than 365 days), tied to the septic/grease question and the Type I hood repair. The LCB license runs 60 to 90 days in parallel and should be filed about 90 days before a realistic opening date.

## TL;DR

- **Kitchen first, liquor second.** Public Health treats a kitchen closed more than 365 days like new construction: a full plan review ($1,008 base for 2026). The property appears to be on septic, not public sewer, so grease handling and septic capacity are the likeliest deal-breakers. The roofless Type I hood needs King County mechanical and fire permits before Public Health will do its preoperational inspection.
- **Alcohol paths:** The Spirits, Beer and Wine Restaurant license ($2,200 to $2,700 a year) gives the most flexibility but requires four complete meals cooked on site and a cook on duty. The Beer/Wine Restaurant license ($600 a year) needs only minimum food service. A Tavern ($600) bars anyone under 21. A Snack Bar ($187.50) has no taps and serves opened bottles only. No true "bridge" license exists for a for-profit business. Special Occasion licenses are for nonprofits only. Banquet permits allow no charge for alcohol and cannot be used once you hold a retail license.
- **Lodging lever:** Four guest rooms at 2 to 4 guests each means up to 16 occupants. That puts the upstairs in transient Group R-1. Capping occupancy at 10 or fewer may let the rooms qualify as a smaller transient lodging house (R-3). Washington's STR law (RCW 64.37) excludes a "duly licensed" inn or hotel. Unincorporated King County has no dedicated STR permit, but zoning for the lodging use must be confirmed.

## Key Findings

1. **Zoning (unconfirmed, likely NB-P).** No official parcel record was reached. A LoopNet table drawing on county tax data lists 24007 Vashon Hwy SW as 3,363 SF on 0.34 acres, zoned "NBP".\[1\] That probably means Neighborhood Business with a P-suffix (property-specific conditions). King County's Vashon-Maury Island subarea plan names Burton as a Rural Neighborhood Commercial Center.\[2\] In those centers, NB uses are capped at 15,000 SF gross floor area, which this building is well under. Confirm the zoning, the P-suffix text, and whether restaurant, tavern and hotel/motel or bed and breakfast uses are permitted outright. A 1980s Vashon plan ordinance mentions a P-condition near Burton limiting some parcels to "a guest inn".\[3\] That may be this site. Verify.
2. **Nonconforming use risk only matters if the restaurant is not permitted outright.** Under KCC 21A.32.025, nonconforming status is forfeited if the use is discontinued beyond KCC 21A.32.045. If a use is discontinued more than twelve months, the applicant must document "no intent to abandon"; "a statement from the property owner that merely states that there is no intent to abandon is not sufficient".\[4\] A roughly five-year gap would almost certainly forfeit nonconforming status. If NB permits restaurants outright (likely, but verify), the gap is irrelevant to zoning.
3. **Not a historic landmark (probably).** The Inn was built in 1993, per island historian Bruce Haulman quoted by the Beachcomber in 2022.\[5\] A third-party listing says 1991.\[6\] The building does not appear on King County landmark lists that include nearby Burton buildings such as the Masonic Hall, the Norman Edson Studio and the Marjesira Inn.\[7\] Not confirmed with the King County Historic Preservation Program.
4. **Sewer: almost certainly septic.** Vashon Sewer District serves only the Town Core, Bunker Trail and Beulah Park/Cove Beach.\[8\] Burton is not in its service area. King County Board of Health Title 13 (updated June 11, 2025) applies:
   - A commercial OSS application must show the sewage does "not exceed typical residential effluent characteristics".\[9\]
   - Grease traps must follow the 2021 Uniform Plumbing Code, and the design must include a maintenance schedule.\[9\]
   - "Expansion" includes extending a building's life expectancy "by being rebuilt, renovated or remodeled".\[9\] Reactivating a dormant restaurant could trigger a septic review.
   - Septic systems serving food facilities must be inspected annually.\[10\]
5. **Water: likely Burton Water Co-op, not Water District 19.** Burton Water Co-op bought Burton Water Company for $1.2 million (the co-op gives October 28, 2024 as the acquisition date; the Beachcomber's October 30, 2024 article says it closed on a "Friday," so verify the date), and per the Beachcomber the company "serves a total of 414 connections (including residential and commercial customers) in the Burton peninsula and surrounding area." Confirm service and any backflow assembly testing it requires.
6. **Codes in force: 2021 codes now, 2024 codes on May 3, 2027.** Washington's 2021 IBC, IEBC, IMC and IFC have been in effect since March 15, 2024.\[11\] The State Building Code Council targets final adoption of the 2024 codes on October 16, 2026, effective May 3, 2027.\[12\] Permits applied for before May 3, 2027 should be reviewed under the 2021 codes. Plan for this.
7. **LCB fees rose in 2025.** 2SSB 5786 (Chapter 343, Laws of 2025, effective July 27, 2025) required that LCB "must increase any liquor license, permit, or endorsement fees set by rule, by 50 percent," and raised the retail application fee 50 percent, per the Legislature's Final Bill Report; statutory restaurant fees rose less, with Spirits/Beer/Wine going from $1,600 to $2,200 (50%+ dining), $2,000 to $2,700 (under 50% dining) and $1,000 to $1,400 (service bar). SB 5448's COVID-era alcohol delivery privileges ended July 1, 2025. Under HB 1698, every server at an on-premises licensee needs a MAST permit and non-servers do not. HB 1515's expanded outdoor service sunsets at the end of 2027. LCB's January 7, 2026 rule package requires to-go cocktails and wine to be "accompanied by a purchased meal".\[13\]

## Details

### A. Zoning and land use

- **Agency:** King County Department of Local Services (DLS), Permitting Division (formerly DPER). Use the Zoning and Historical Certification Letter (available through MyBuildingPermit.com) to get a written zoning answer.\[14\] Request a pre-application meeting for the full scope.
- **Lodging use:** KCC 21A.08 lists "Hotel/Motel" and "Bed and Breakfast Guesthouse" as separate uses.\[15\] A 2022 King County zoning study table shows B&B guesthouse as "Permitted" in one comparison zone and hotel/motel as "Not Allowed" or "Conditional" depending on zone.\[16\] Do not assume hotel/motel is allowed in NB in a rural neighborhood center. Get this in writing.
- **B&B guesthouse limits:** One secondary source summarizes unincorporated King County as allowing "only a bed-and-breakfast guesthouse accessory to the operator's residence, ≤5 rooms".\[17\] If that residency condition is correct, it is a problem for an off-site operator.
- **Parking (KCC 21A.18):** B&B guesthouse needs 1 space per guest room plus 2 per facility (6 total for 4 rooms). Hotel/motel needs 1 per bedroom.\[15\]\[18\] The restaurant adds its own requirement. Verify the existing on-site count.
- **Signs:** NB zone signs are limited by KCC 21A.20.095.\[15\] A building permit is required for most signs.\[15\]
- **STR rules:** Unincorporated King County has no STR registration or permit program. Two 2026 secondary sources say so directly;\[17\]\[19\] one low-quality site claims otherwise and was not relied on.\[20\] State law RCW 64.37 still applies. It defines an STR as a lodging use "that is not a hotel or motel or bed and breakfast", and excludes a "duly licensed bed and breakfast, inn, hotel, motel".\[21\] Operators must carry at least $1,000,000 liability coverage (or rely on equivalent platform coverage) and post safety and contact information.\[17\]

### B. Building, fire and mechanical

- **Occupancy (IBC 2021 Section 310, inferred from the model code):**
  - R-1 covers transient boarding houses with more than 10 occupants.
  - R-3 covers transient lodging houses with five or fewer guest rooms and 10 or fewer occupants.
  - 4 rooms at up to 4 guests (16 total) is R-1. Capping total guests at 10 is the cheapest way to stay R-3.
  - Downstairs, a 40-seat dining room with an occupant load under 50 is generally Group B, not A-2. Fifty or more makes it A-2, which raises egress and fire alarm demands.
- **Change of occupancy:** If the building was permitted in 1993 as an inn with a restaurant, renting the same rooms nightly is the same occupancy, and IEBC repair rules apply. Confirm with the original permit and Certificate of Occupancy.
- **Hood and exhaust:** Replacing the removed roof fan and reactivating the Type I hood is mechanical work. Expect:
  - a King County mechanical permit (IMC 507 hood, makeup air, upblast fan, grease duct per NFPA 96);
  - a fire suppression permit for a UL 300 wet chemical system, reviewed by the King County Fire Marshal;
  - a Washington L&I electrical permit (inferred; L&I handles electrical in unincorporated areas);
  - Public Health plumbing permits for sinks and the grease interceptor (inferred).

  With no equipment documents, assume the hood listing, the duct's clearance to combustibles and the suppression system all have to be recertified or replaced.
- **Fire alarm and sprinklers:** Under IEBC, a repair or Level 1 alteration does not trigger retroactive sprinklers. A change of occupancy, or a Level 2/3 alteration over the work-area thresholds, can. Ask King County whether smoke alarms in each guest room and in the means of egress are adequate for the occupancy as permitted.
- **CO alarms:** Required where there are fuel-burning appliances or an attached garage. The 2024 IBC amendment WAC 51-50-0915 is in the new package.\[22\]
- **Accessibility (WAC 51-50 Chapter 11 and IEBC):**
  - Repairs do not trigger accessibility upgrades.
  - Alterations to primary-function areas trigger an accessible route to the altered area. Spending on that route can be capped at 20 percent of alteration cost (the IEBC "disproportionate cost" rule).
  - "Technically infeasible" exceptions apply where structure prevents compliance.
  - The federal ADA lodging exemption covers only five or fewer rooms "actually occupied by the proprietor as a residence". It likely does not apply here unless the operator lives on site.
  - Upstairs-only rooms are a known liability. Document a feasibility analysis.
- **Fire marshal:** Vashon Island Fire & Rescue publishes no commercial plan review, operational permit or annual inspection fee schedule.\[23\] Its notices cite the King County Fire Marshal (DLS) for unincorporated Vashon.\[24\] Plan on King County Fire Marshal review and inspection, with fees from the King County permit fee schedule.\[14\] King County DLS states that "effective January 1, 2025, most permit-related fees… increased by 49%" under Ordinance 19857 (approved November 19, 2024), and Ordinance 20021 (enacted November 25, 2025) was proposed to raise permit fees a further 11.8 percent plus a new 3.5 percent temporary development surcharge for 2026; it passed in amended form, so check the final 2026 rates.

### C. Water, sewer and grease

- No public sewer is likely. Order an OSS as-built from Public Health's online OSS records search, and a condition inspection by a King County certified On-site System Maintainer.
- Restaurant wastewater is high strength. Title 13 requires commercial designs to show residential-strength effluent,\[9\] so an exterior grease interceptor (or a large in-ground grease trap) ahead of the septic tank is the standard fix, subject to setbacks.
- Public Health's own authority covers systems up to 3,500 gallons per day.\[9\] A 40-seat restaurant plus 4 guest rooms should fall well within that.
- Water: confirm Burton Water Co-op service, meter size and backflow (premises isolation for commercial kitchens is common).

### D. Public Health (PHSKC)

- **When plan review is required:** for a food service establishment that "has been closed for more than 365 days and will reopen". If closed 90 to 365 days, a "Field Plan Review" applies (January 2026 tip sheet).\[25\]
- **Fees:** New construction is a 4-hour base. The application form describes it as "re-permitting an existing kitchen that has not been used for one (1) year or more".\[26\] The current guide lists $1,008 base, the remodel $756, and $252 per extra hour.\[27\]\[28\] An older form shows $972, $729 and $243; budget the higher figures.\[26\]
  - One preoperational inspection is included. Re-inspections cost extra. Plan review fees are non-refundable.\[25\]
- **Annual operating permit ("through 2026" schedule), General Food Service, 13 to 50 seats:**
  - Risk 1: $441\[28\]
  - Risk 2: $756\[28\]
  - Risk 3: $1,008\[28\]

  A full cook-chill or multi-step menu will likely be Risk 3. A simpler menu may be Risk 2.
- **What to submit:** site plan, equipment floor plan, equipment list or schedule, menu, and plan review questionnaire.\[29\]
- **Equipment the plan must show (standard WA Food Code items, inferred):** handwashing sinks in each prep area, a 3-compartment sink, a food prep sink, a mop sink, refrigeration capacity, and the hood.
- **People:** Food worker cards for all food handlers. Confirm Public Health's current Certified Food Protection Manager expectation. Not verified in this research.
- **STR guests:** With no food served, Public Health has no lodging permit role found for 4 rooms. Verify whether any transient accommodation licensing applies at this size.

### E. LCB licensing

**Process (LCB Guide and Licensing Process pages):**
1. File through DOR Business Licensing Service. Apply no more than 90 days before opening. You need a business address.\[30\]
2. A licensing specialist contacts you within about a week. Phase 1 takes about 10 days.\[31\]\[32\]
3. LCB notifies the local authority (King County). The county has 20 days to respond, or 60 days in an Alcohol Impact Area.\[32\] Burton is not one, but verify.
4. A public notice is posted at the premises for 14 days.\[30\]
5. You submit documents: business structure, financing and start-up funding sources, lease, and floor plan.\[32\]
6. On approval, an approval letter acts as a 30-day license until the business license with the liquor endorsement arrives.\[32\]

LCB's enforcement FAQ says applications "typically take 60-90 days for an in-state license".\[33\] Fingerprinting and criminal history checks for principals are standard. Confirm current fingerprint triggers with your specialist.

**Entity:** Burton Inn LLC, with every member and manager (and spouses, under LCB rules) disclosed as a true party of interest. Landlords are generally not principals, but a percentage-of-sales rent should be reviewed with LCB.

**Landlord:** LCB needs a lease or sublease showing premises control. Expect the specialist to ask for a copy. A landlord consent letter is advisable but is not a confirmed LCB requirement.

### F. Business registration and taxes

- **Business license:** DOR Business License with the liquor endorsement under one UBI for Burton Inn LLC (WAC 458-02-200(7) sets the "business license application handling fee to open the first business location of a new business, or to reopen a closed business" at $50, non-refundable per DOR form 700028). Unincorporated King County has no general city business license.\[34\]
- **Lodging taxes:** Stays under 30 days carry retail sales tax, local sales tax, and King County's convention and trade center tax\[17\] (2.8 percent outside Seattle).\[35\]\[36\] DOR confirms Airbnb collects and remits "state and local retail sales tax", "special hotel/motel taxes" and "convention and trade center taxes".\[37\] The LLC must still register, file excise returns, report gross lodging income, take the facilitator deduction, and pay Retailing B&O.\[37\]\[38\]\[39\]
- **Restaurant and bar:** You collect and remit sales tax yourself.
- **Music:** ASCAP/BMI/SESAC licenses are needed for recorded or live music (optional).

**Partner's mobile bar:** LCB liquor caterer licenses are "prohibited from catering events at liquor-licensed premises."\[41\] Once the Inn is licensed, the partner's separate business cannot pour at the Inn under its own license. Partner staff can work events as Burton Inn LLC employees with MAST permits, using the Inn's inventory.

**Structure options:**
- **(a) Burton Inn LLC holds the license and operates.** Simplest. The landlord's lease must allow it.
- **(b) The operator (partner's company) subleases the kitchen and bar and holds the license.** The operator needs its own lease or sublease, UBI and Public Health permit, and Burton Inn LLC cannot share in alcohol revenue without being disclosed.
- **(c) Management agreement with Burton Inn LLC as licensee.** Must be disclosed and reviewed by LCB.

**Alcohol in rooms:** A restaurant license covers only the floor-plan premises. Welcome bottles bundled into a paid stay are a likely unlicensed sale. Do not do it without written LCB guidance.

## Tab 4: Kitchen reactivation checklist

1. Inventory all remaining equipment: make, model, NSF/UL labels.
2. Hood: get the UL listing and a duct inspection. Replace the roof upblast fan. Install makeup air. Have a NFPA 96 cleaning certificate issued.
3. Suppression: install a new or recertified UL 300 system with fuel shutoff and a K-class extinguisher.
4. Sinks: handwashing sinks, 3-compartment sink, prep sink, mop sink. Check water heater capacity.
5. Grease: exterior interceptor sized to UPC 2021, with a maintenance schedule filed with the septic design.
6. Septic: OSM inspection and flow and strength review.
7. Refrigeration and thermometers; finishes that are smooth and cleanable.
8. Floor plan and menu to Public Health. Set the risk level.
9. Food worker cards; Certified Food Protection Manager.
10. Preoperational inspection; operating permit posted.
